What determines the sign?

Within and between weights

Let r be the within-weight correlation and h the fraction of the variance of each of X₁ and X₂ explained by weight. As weight rises, the mean of exercise falls and the mean of bone density rises when h > 0.

Dividing the law of total covariance by the common marginal variance gives

ρmarginal = (1 − h)r − h.

For positive r, reversal occurs when h exceeds r/(1 + r).

The Gaussian model

All three variables are centred. Write A = Var(X₁) = Var(X₂) = 7/3, v = Var(X₃) and t = X₃/√v. At fixed t, the conditional mean is (−t√(Ah), t√(Ah)). The conditional variances are A(1 − h) and the conditional covariance is Ar(1 − h).

How to read the contours

Probability inside the curve

A Gaussian distribution extends beyond every ellipse shown here. A 90% contour encloses 90% of the probability under its own distribution. The density is highest at the centre. A line itself has probability zero. Switch on Sample points to see draws both inside and outside the curves.

The straight lines show the regression of bone density on exercise. The dots at their centres mark the means.

Projecting and slicing a shell

The grey ellipsoids are surfaces of constant joint density. Their blue footprints give the marginal contours. The percentages on the right refer to the two-dimensional distributions. At mean weight the orange sections coincide in shape and size with the conditional contours on the right. Away from the mean, a plane can miss an inner shell entirely.

A right-panel contour enclosing probability p has squared Mahalanobis radius q = −2 log(1 − p). The matching joint shell has squared radius q. At standardised weight t its section has squared conditional radius q − t² if this is nonnegative. The right panel keeps q fixed for each probability.
